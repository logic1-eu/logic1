# SMT-LIB DNF benchmark notes

Updated: 2026-11-06

This document covers `benchmarks/theories/RCF/smtlib/` and all its descendants,
including `inputs/`, excluding sibling benchmark directories. It records
provenance, selection criteria, measurements, and completed verification for
that subtree. Operational instructions are in this directory's `AGENTS.md`;
`NOTES.md` is supporting documentation, not an automatically loaded agent
instruction file.

## Original selection

The first six candidates were selected by source structure before running
the methods. A pilot run found that both native methods reached 30 seconds
on two Ultimate cases. We retained `half-2` as a high-variable stress case,
removed the similar `count_by_k` case, and added three smaller Uncu inputs
to make the timing comparison useful across more workload sizes. The final
eight-input selection follows these criteria:

1. belong to QF_NRA and contain only real polynomial comparisons;
2. use conjunctions containing disjunctions, so DNF construction must
   distribute rather than merely copy an existing DNF;
3. use only the explicitly supported SMT-LIB operators in `smtlib_inputs.py`;
4. span small through substantial raw Cartesian products (4 to 2,048);
5. include two independently generated source families; and
6. are small enough to keep and inspect as original input files.

tree: an atom contributes one, `or` adds child counts, and `and` multiplies
them. It counts combinations before duplicate removal or any algebraic or
Boolean simplification. The count is a structural workload indicator, not
an expected output size. The selection is deliberately varied and is not a
statistical sample of all QF_NRA benchmarks.

### Search for differences between simplification options

A bounded Sage screen examined 7,326 distinct inputs: all 7,006 MetiTarski
inputs, all 245 Pine inputs, and all 75 Uncu files with disjunctive denominator
simplifications. Each input received a fresh polynomial ring and cleared
caches. The screen first preprocessed the formula and compared the strict
DNFs after syntactic reduction. It ran the full computations with the default
`max_pair_checks=500` when those strict DNFs differed.

| Screening outcome | Inputs |
| --- | ---: |
| Preprocessing leaves at most one Cartesian product | 6,435 |
| Identical strict DNFs for FINAL, AFTER_STEP, and DURING_STEP | 830 |
| Different strict DNFs, but identical final formulas with default options | 59 |
| Exploratory timeout | 2 |

The two exploratory timeouts were the existing sumcracker-14d and
sumcracker-14e cases. The exploratory guard was three seconds, with a
30-second guard for promising full comparisons; these observations are not
substitutes for the benchmark's 30-second measurements. Equal-count strict
DNFs were also checked for structural equality. No final-size difference
between the three simplifying options was found in the completed screen.

The 59 intermediate differences occur in Polypaver square-root proof
obligations. The selected `polypaver-0326` has 48 products after preprocessing.
Syntactic reduction leaves seven cubes with 48 atoms under FINAL and
AFTER_STEP, and three cubes with 20 atoms under DURING_STEP. All three public
computations return two cubes with 14 atoms at the default comparison limit.
A separate check of the intermediate collections establishes that final
semantic subsumption removes this difference. With `max_pair_checks=0`, the
public results instead retain the respective seven and three cubes (48 and
20 atoms); conservative final simplification does not change those counts.
This is a practical example of different intermediate reductions and of
different final sizes when semantic subsumption is disabled. It is not a
witness of different final sizes with the default options.

Additional probes selected `lex-index` as a moderate verification workload:
`Simplification.FINAL` returns eight cubes with 304 atoms. The larger case
`InVarSynth_NutzTransformation05.c_Iteration1_0.smt2`, timed out at 30 seconds
under all three simplifying options and was not added. Small hycomp inputs
with free Boolean declarations and ezsmt inputs using `ite` were excluded
because they exceed the polynomial reader's supported fragment.

### Nonlinear algebraic dependencies

The following relationships explain the choices for future Gröbner
simplification. They are mathematical properties of the source formulas;
they are not claims that the current `gsimplify` already proves them.

- **Pine:** `pine-quadratic` connects the invariant before and after
  `x' = x + 0.01*(-x + 2*x*x + y*y)` and
  `y' = y + 0.01*(-y + y*y)`. The transition equations relate the
  polynomials on both sides of the inductiveness query. `pine-pendulum`
  uses the degree-five approximation `u - u**3/6 + u**5/120` in a pendulum
  step and includes bounded rounding-error variables. Both inputs have
  five branches for violating the next-state invariant.
- **Regular pentagon:** the retained equations include
  `P = v7**2 + v8**2 - 2*v7 = 0` and
  `Q = 4*v7**2 - 6*v7 + 1 = 0`. They imply the quartic constraint
  `16*v8**4 - 20*v8**2 + 5 = 0`, certified by
  `16*v8**4 - 20*v8**2 + 5 = Q + (4*P - Q)*(4*v8**2 + 2*v7 - 4)`.
  Further equations connect these coordinates to the diagonal/side ratio.
- **Triangle:** write `u=v5`, `w=v6`, `a=v7`, and `b=v8`. The equations
  `P = u**2 + w**2 - a**2 - 2*u + 1 = 0` and
  `Q = u**2 + w**2 - b**2 = 0` imply
  `(b**2 - a**2 + 1)**2 + 4*w**2 - 4*b**2 = 0`. Its left-hand side is
  identically `4*Q + (P-Q)*(b**2 - a**2 + 1 + 2*u)`.
  The input also has `w*v10 = 1` and a cubic ratio equation.
- **Economics 0016c:** after the source's explicit substitutions, its
  supply-demand equations imply `(v11-v12)*v6 = v1` and
  `(v11-v12)*v5 = 0`. The assumptions `v11 < 0 < v12` and `v1 > 0`
  give `v5 = 0` and `v6 < 0`. Combining different equations is necessary
  to establish these consequences. Negating the economic hypothesis gives
  three branches.
- **Economics 0063c:** let the five equation left-hand sides be
  `E1=v7*v9-v3`, `E2=v7*v8*v9-v2*v7-v10*v4*v8`, `E3=v10*v8-v1`,
  `E4=v3*v6*v8-v2*v7`, and `E5=v3*v5*v8-v1*v4`. The identity
  `v3*v8*(v5+v6-1) = E4+E5-E2+v8*E1-v4*E3` shows that the five equations,
  together with `v3 > 0` and `v8 > 0`, force the factor shares to sum to one. This
  contradicts the asserted negation of that conclusion. It is a compact
  case combining ideal membership with nonzero assumptions.

The three polynomial identities displayed for the pentagon, triangle, and
economics-0063c were checked by exact expansion to zero with Sage. This
validates the stated certificates; it is not an independent equivalence
check of all computed DNFs.

### Algebraic structure of the additions

These observations describe the source formulas, without claiming measured
improvements from Gröbner simplification.

**Ultimate invariant synthesis** also contributes nonlinear algebraic
structure. The four selected files contain bilinear products of shared
invariant-template coefficients and Motzkin multipliers. Their equations
and inequalities relate these products across constraints. The equations
occur within the Boolean formulas; they must not all be treated as global
assumptions. Thus these inputs combine Boolean distribution with polynomial
dependencies, rather than supplying only a large number of branches.

**Economics 1001c** concerns constant absolute risk aversion with states
delineated. It has 35 asserted nonlinear equations. To expose a useful
relationship, write `a=v1`, `b=v2`, `x_j=v(3+j)`, `w_j=v(36+j)`, and
`t_j=v(69+j)` for `j=0,...,32`; here `v(k)` denotes the source variable `vk`.
The equations include `t_j=-a*w_j` and `sum_j w_j*(x_j-1)=0`. Together they
imply `sum_j t_j*(x_j-1)=0`. Another source equation is
`sum_j t_j*((x_j-1)+b*(x_j-1)**2)=0`, which therefore gives `b*Q=0` for
`Q=sum_j t_j*(x_j-1)**2`. The source also asserts `Q<0` and `b!=0`, so these
relationships yield a contradiction. This combines polynomial equations
with a sign constraint, rather than relying only on high degree.

**Kissing 4-14 and 4-25** use respectively 14 and 25 vectors `x_i` in four
dimensions. Each vector satisfies `||x_i||**2=1`, and every pair satisfies
`||x_i-x_j||**2>=1`. There are 14 or 25 norm equations and 91 or 300
separation inequalities. Modulo the norm equations, each separation
inequality becomes `2*dot(x_i,x_j)<=1`, using the Euclidean dot product.
Thus equations connect the coordinate squares to inequalities
linking different vectors. All polynomials are quadratic.

**Kochański dodecagon** has 26 equations, including 24 quadratic equations,
for a regular dodecagon, intersections of lines through its vertices, and
a segment-length ratio. For example, the equations
`P=v7**2+v8**2-2*v7=0` and `Q=4*v7**2-8*v7+1=0` imply `4*v8**2-1=0`
through the identity `4*P-Q=4*v8**2-1`. Coordinate recurrences and further
intersection equations link these coordinates to the distance equation
`v32**2=(v27-v29)**2+v30**2`, with `m=v32` and `v33=1`.

**MGC10** has four nonlinear equations sharing nine variables, with total
degree up to 23. Three equations are `theta*H=0`,
`gamma0*mu+lambda1*vv1-vv2=0`, and `10*alpha*H+delta*(vv2-vv3)=0`, where
`H=gamma0-vv1*(vv3**10+1)`. Positivity of `theta` and `delta` implies `H=0`
and `vv2=vv3`, hence `vv1*(mu*(vv3**10+1)+lambda1)=vv3`. The fourth equation
and remaining inequalities constrain the same variables. This supplies
higher-degree relationships between several equations, contrasting with
MBO's single large polynomial.

**pPDA mod5 and mod7** encode recursive programs differing in the modulus
used for one recursive argument. Although their source metadata describes
polynomial equations, the retained no-hints files actually assert
inequalities `p_i>=0` and `p_i>=P_i(p)`. The polynomials `P_i` contain rational
linear combinations and products of other probabilities, whose constraints
create cyclic dependencies. These are substantial nonlinear inequality
systems, but they contain no asserted equations for a Gröbner basis.

**MBO E7E10** asks whether a degree-20 polynomial has a zero with all seven
variables positive. Its original file is 254,304 bytes and contains mixed
coefficient signs. It adds a large polynomial with few atoms, complementing
the larger numbers of small polynomials above. A single equation does not
supply the same kind of algebraic dependencies as the Economics system.

## Search for schedule differences with a 90-second limit

On 2026-11-04 the main computation limit was raised from 30 to 90 seconds.
The objective was to add up to eight original SMT-LIB inputs whose returned
DNFs have different atom counts under `FINAL`, `AFTER_STEP`, and
`DURING_STEP`, with the unchanged default `max_pair_checks=500`. Completed
30-second measurements remain part of the current benchmark set; a larger
limit does not invalidate them. Startup and output counting retain their

## Translation and measurement

`smtlib_inputs.py` reads one original SMT-LIB file in each worker before
benchmark timing. This prevents variables from other cases entering the
worker's polynomial ring.
It accepts real declarations, assertions, `and`, `or`, `not`, Boolean
constants, polynomial addition, subtraction and multiplication, division
by nonzero constants, and binary comparisons. Lexical `let` bindings can
hold polynomials or formulas; all right-hand sides use the outer environment
before any new binding becomes visible. Nullary `define-fun` definitions are
evaluated in declaration order and checked against their declared sorts.
Free Boolean variables, functions with arguments, `ite`, and division by
variable or zero denominators are rejected. Decimal numerals and constant
quotients are represented exactly, and every comparison `left OP right`
becomes the equivalent
`left - right OP 0`, as required by Logic1's RCF simplifier. Sage does not
accept some Ultimate variable names containing `.`
and `~`; those names are renamed bijectively to safe names. Logic1 flattens
adjacent operators of the same kind when building `And` and `Or`. The same
resulting Logic1 formula is supplied to all configurations.

The main comparison has six configurations: Redlog DNF with both `bnfsac`
and `bnfsm` off or on, and `method="cartesian"` with `Simplification.NONE`,
`FINAL`, `AFTER_STEP`, or `DURING_STEP`. Explicit `NONE` retains ordinary
preprocessing and conservative final simplification. Other schedules use
`max_pair_checks=500`. Since 2026-10-23, the public defaults are Cartesian
construction and `FINAL`; explicit benchmark options preserve the recorded
configurations. Every Cartesian schedule preprocesses with
`explode_always=False, substitute=1`. In the historical measurements, early
means `DURING_STEP` and subtree means `AFTER_STEP`.
Each main-comparison case starts a fresh worker and times one call. The
recorded wall limit was 600 seconds for the 16 inputs then in `LONG_CASES`
and 60 seconds for the other main cases. New runs take their limit solely
from `--dnf-timeout` (infinity by default). Completed measurements made with
earlier limits remain valid for the current benchmark set. It counts
output atoms after timing.
Worker startup, SMT-LIB parsing, and output counting are outside the measured
interval. The timed call includes the small parent/worker pipe round trip,
as in the motor benchmark. Each worker has its own process group, which is
killed on timeout.
Startup and result counting have separate 30-second guards. A computation timeout is an expected outcome with an unknown result size,
not a failure or a zero-atom result. Startup and validation timeouts remain
failures. Timed runs
use one round, one iteration, and no warmup. Runs were sequential except
for the 600-second lower-table rerun, which used two concurrent calls.

The full five-method run on 2026-10-21 completed 36 of 40 computations.
All three Cartesian methods completed `half-2` and returned 280 atom
occurrences, compared with 288 from each Redlog method. Both Redlog methods
and both simplified Cartesian methods timed out on `sumcracker-14e`; the
Cartesian method completed it. The two measured tables are in
`dnf_results.md`. Atom counts describe result size; this
benchmark does not perform a separate equivalence check. This historical
40-case run precedes the supplementary cases described below.

## Supplementary experiments

### Initial implicit ranges on levin-rhsc

The additional case uses `method="cartesian"` and `Simplification.DURING_STEP`, with
`implicit_ranges=False` only during initial simplification. It uses a
temporary replacement of `CartesianBnf.simplify` inside the
worker and restores it afterwards. Semantic implication checks and final
simplification retain their defaults. The current computation limit is
60 seconds; the recorded measurements below used 30 seconds.

On 2026-10-21, early returned 88 atom occurrences in 0.299 seconds with the
default setting and 16 in 0.209 seconds with initial implicit ranges disabled.
Enabling the option reduces the initial formula but collapses explicit
variable sign cases. The current simplifier cannot recover those signs from
product constraints, leaving six contradictory cubes. This is the only
observed case so far where enabling implicit ranges increases the final atom
count; other completed cases in the motor and SMT-LIB experiment had equal
or smaller counts with the option enabled. The default remains enabled.

Equivalence of the two levin-rhsc outputs was checked separately from timing.
Every default-output cube contains `V2 != 0`; splitting each cube on `V2 < 0`
and `V2 > 0` is therefore exhaustive. Simplifying this disjunction produced a
formula structurally identical to the output with initial implicit ranges
disabled. This check used the RCF simplifier, not an independent solver.

### Pair-check budgets on sumcracker-14e

The separate benchmark doubles `max_pair_checks` from 1 until the first
timeout, independently for early and subtree. It also has zero-budget
controls. The computation limit is 60 seconds, with the default initial
`implicit_ranges=True`; startup and counting guards remain 30 seconds.

Both sweeps completed budgets 1 through 1024 and first timed out at 2048.
Every completed run, including both zero-budget controls, returned 34,816
atom occurrences. The zero-budget times were 44.241 seconds for early and
41.600 for subtree; at budget 1024 they were 58.292 and 56.887 seconds.
Plain cartesian had returned the same count in 13.841 seconds in the main
comparison. Setting the budget to zero disables final semantic comparisons;
the local subsumption and cut steps still run, so it does not reproduce the
Cartesian algorithm. Increasing the budget gave no final size improvement
on this input. Selected budget results are in `dnf_results.md`.
